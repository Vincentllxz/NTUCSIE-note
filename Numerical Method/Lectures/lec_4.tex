\section{Condition of Matrices}

\subsection{Vector and Matrix Norms}

To calculate the error, we can separate into 3 types: scalar, vector, and matrix. For scalar, we can simply use
\begin{itemize}
    \item absolute error: \[
        |\tilde{\alpha} - \alpha|
    \]
    \item relative error: \[
        \frac{|\tilde{\alpha} - \alpha|}{|\alpha|}
    \]
\end{itemize}

To evaluate the vector and matrix error, we need to define the vector and matrix norms, which is a kind of metric. 

\begin{definition*}[Vector Norm]
    Here are some common vector norms:
    \begin{definition}[$l$-norm]
        \[
            \|x\|_l = \left(\sum_{i=1}^n |x_i|^l\right)^{\frac{1}{l}}
        \]
    \end{definition}
    for instance, 
    \begin{definition}[$1$-norm]
        \[
            \|x\|_1 = \sum_{i=1}^n |x_i|
        \]
    \end{definition}

    \begin{definition}[$\infty$-norm]
        \[
            \|x\|_\infty \equiv \lim_{l\to\infty} \|x\|_l
        \]
    \end{definition}
\end{definition*}

\begin{theorem}
    \[
        \|x\|_\infty = \max_{1\leq i \leq n} |x_i|
    \]    
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    We can induce by Squeeze Theorem. For any $l$, we have \[
        \sqrt[l]{(\max |x_i|)^{l}} \leq \sqrt[l]{|x_1|^{l} + \cdots + |x_n|^l} \leq \sqrt[l]{n(\max |x_i|)^{l}}
    \]
    which implies \[
        \max |x_i| \leq \|x\|_l \leq n^{\frac{1}{l}} \max |x_i|
    \]
    By Squeeze Theorem ($l \to \infty$), we have \[
        \|x\|_\infty = \max |x_i|
    \] 
    Proof is complete.
\end{proof}

\begin{eg}
    \[
        x = \begin{pmatrix}
            1 \\ 1000 \\ 9
        \end{pmatrix}, \quad 
        x = \begin{pmatrix}
            1.1 \\ 99 \\ 11
        \end{pmatrix}
    \]
\end{eg}

\[
    \|\hat{x} - x\|_\infty = 2, \quad \frac{\|\hat{x} - x\|_\infty}{\|x\|_\infty} = 0.02, \quad \frac{\|\hat{x} - x\|_\infty}{\|\hat{x}\|_\infty} = 0.0202
\]
\[
    \|\hat{x} - x\|_2 = 2.238, \quad \frac{\|\hat{x} - x\|_2}{\|x\|_2} = 0.0223, \quad \frac{\|\hat{x} - x\|_2}{\|\hat{x}\|_2} = 0.0225
\]

\begin{corollary}
    For $l$-norm, all norms are equivalent to each other, which means there exist $c_1, c_2 > 0$ such that \[
        c_1 \|x\|_{l_1} \leq \|x\|_{l_2} \leq c_2 \|x\|_{l_1}
    \]
    for all $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$.
\end{corollary}

For instance, \[
    \|x\|_2 \leq \|x\|_1 \leq \sqrt{n} \|x\|_2 \tag{2.3.1}
\]
\[
    \|x\|_\infty \leq \|x\|_2 \leq \sqrt{n} \|x\|_\infty \tag{2.3.2}
\]
\[
    \|x\|_\infty \leq \|x\|_1 \leq n \|x\|_\infty \tag{2.3.3}
\]

\begin{theorem}[norm]
    A norm should satisfy \[
        \|A\| \geq 0
    \]\[
        \|A + B\| \leq \|A\| + \|B\| \tag{2.3.4}
    \]\[
        \|\alpha A\| = |\alpha| \|A\|
    \]
    where $\alpha$ is a scalar.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    Checking the middle one, we have \[
        \|A+B\| = \max_{\|x\| = 1} \|(A+B)x\| \leq \max_{\|x\| = 1} \|Ax\| + \|Bx\| \leq \max_{\|x\| = 1} \|Ax\| + \max_{\|x\| = 1} \|Bx\|
    \]
    The last step is because we can choose different $x$ to maximize $\|Ax\|$ and $\|Bx\|$.
\end{proof}

Hence, here we have the definition of matrix norm:

\begin{definition}[Matrix Norm]
    A matrix norm is a function $\|\cdot\|$ \[
        \|A\|_l \equiv \max_{x \neq 0} \frac{\|Ax\|_l}{\|x\|_l} = \max_{\|x\|_l = 1} \|Ax\|_l
    \]
\end{definition}

From the definition, above we have \[
    \frac{\|Ax\|}{\|x\|} \leq \|A\|,\quad \forall x
\]
so \[
    \|Ax\| \leq \|A\| \|x\|
\]

\begin{note}

    For the relative error, \[
        \frac{|\hat{\alpha} - \alpha|}{|\alpha|}
    \]
    but in practice, $\alpha$ is usually unknown, so we can only use \[
        \frac{|\hat{\alpha} - \alpha|}{|\hat{\alpha}|}
    \]
    
    If \[
        \frac{|\hat{\alpha} - \alpha|}{|\hat{\alpha}|} \leq 0.1
    \]
    then \[
        \frac{1}{1.1} \frac{|\hat{\alpha} - \alpha|}{|\hat{\alpha}|} \leq \frac{|\hat{\alpha} - \alpha|}{|\alpha|} \leq \frac{1}{0.9} \frac{|\hat{\alpha} - \alpha|}{|\hat{\alpha}|}
    \]
    By \[
        |\alpha| - |\hat{\alpha}| \leq |\hat{\alpha} - \alpha| \leq 0.1 |\hat{\alpha}|
    \]
    \[
        |\alpha| \leq 1.1 |\hat{\alpha}|
    \]
    Similarly, \[
        |\hat{\alpha}| - |\alpha| \leq 0.1 |\hat{\alpha}|
    \]
    \[
        |\alpha| \geq 0.9 |\hat{\alpha}|
    \]
\end{note}

\subsection{Condition of a Linear System}

Solving a Linear System \[
    \begin{pmatrix}
        10 & 7 & 8 & 7 \\
        7 & 5 & 6 & 5 \\
        8 & 6 & 10 & 9 \\
        7 & 5 & 9 & 10
    \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix}
        x_1 \\ x_2 \\ x_3 \\ x_4
    \end{pmatrix}
    =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        32 \\ 23 \\ 33\\ 31
    \end{pmatrix}, \mbox{ solution } =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        1 \\ 1 \\ 1 \\ 1
    \end{pmatrix}
\]
Now we slightly modify the right-hand side to \[
    \begin{pmatrix}
        10 & 7 & 8 & 7 \\
        7 & 5 & 6 & 5 \\
        8 & 6 & 10 & 9 \\
        7 & 5 & 9 & 10
    \end{pmatrix}
    \begin{pmatrix}
        x_1 \\ x_2 \\ x_3 \\ x_4
    \end{pmatrix}
    =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        32.1 \\ 22.9 \\ 33.1 \\ 30.9
    \end{pmatrix}, \mbox{ solution } =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        9.2 \\ -12.6 \\ 4.5 \\ -1.1
    \end{pmatrix}
\]
We can see that \red{a small change can lead to a large error}. Or if we slightly modify the coefficient matrix \[
    \begin{pmatrix}
        10 & 7 & 8.1 & 7.2 \\
        7.08 & 5.04 & 6 & 5 \\
        8 & 5.98 & 9.89 & 9 \\
        6.99 & 4.99 & 9 & 9.98
    \end{pmatrix}
    \begin{pmatrix}
        x_1 \\ x_2 \\ x_3 \\ x_4
    \end{pmatrix}
    =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        32 \\ 23 \\ 33\\ 31
    \end{pmatrix}
    \mbox{, solution } =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        -81 \\ 137 \\ -34 \\ 22
    \end{pmatrix}
\]
which is even worse.

Now, we consider the gereral case \[
    Ax = b
\]
\begin{enumerate}[label=$\arabic*^\circ$]
    \item First, we slightly modify right-hand side \[
        A(x + \delta x) = b + \delta b
    \]
    then we get \[
        \delta x = A^{-1} \delta b \quad \implies \quad \|\delta x\| \leq \|A^{-1}\| \|\delta b\|
    \]
    and we have \[
        \|b\| \leq \|A\| \|x\|
    \]
    so \[
        \boxed{\frac{\|\delta x\|}{\|x\|} \leq \|A^{-1}\| \|A\| \frac{\|\delta b\|}{\|b\|}}
    \]
    
    \item Next, we slightly modify the coefficient matrix \[
        (A + \delta A)(x + \delta x) = b
    \]\[
        Ax + A \delta x + \delta A (x + \delta x) = b
    \]
    then we have \[
        \delta x = -A^{-1} \delta A (x + \delta x) 
    \]
    imply the norm \[
        \|\delta x\| \leq \|A^{-1}\| \|\delta A\| (\|x\| + \|\delta x\|)
    \]
    so \[
        \boxed{\frac{\|\delta x\|}{\|x + \delta x\|} \leq \|A\|\|A^{-1}\| \frac{\|\delta A\|} {\|A\|}}
    \]
    \begin{note}
        We here estimate only \[
            \frac{\|\delta x\|}{\|x + \delta x\|}
            \text{ instead of }
            \frac{\|\delta x\|}{\|x\|};
        \]
        since the disscussion earlier.
    \end{note}
\end{enumerate}

Clearly, the error is related to $\|A\|\|A^{-1}\|$, which is called the \red{condition number} of $A$, a smaller condition number means a more stable system.
